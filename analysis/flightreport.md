all four engines ignited at once, however high winds caused a very parabolic flight path. roughly one second past apogee, the ejection charge from the engines pushed off the nose cone but the parachute failed to fully open up, resulting in a high impact velocity, as well as resulting in a lot of rotation of the avionics bay.

the acceleration calculated during flight seems fairly accurate as it clearly indicates a strong initial acceleration, followed by a consistent negative velocity hovering around 10m/s^2, until the calculated acceleration becomes extremely erratic when the parachute fails to fully open.

OpenRocket predicted an apogee at only 125 meters due to the heavy weight of the 9v battery and avionics bay enclosure compared to the thrust provided from the c6 rocket engines. if this was achieved, the final velocity integrated from acceleration values are far from accurate.

due to the orientation of the mpu6050, it is highly unlikely the pitch or roll went above 360 degrees, as the rocket itself had only turned 180 degrees from the vertical axis by the end of the flight

the post flight analysis left much to be corrected, but has proved the acceleration calculation is fairly accurate and reliable.
